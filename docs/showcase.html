02Convoy

        

          One client, one server, ten requests, and a packet lost every few seconds. Switch the
          transport and watch what one loss costs the others: on HTTP/2 the streams are meant to be
          independent, but they share a connection, which is why HTTP/3 exists.
        

        

          
          

            
            
            
            
            
            
          

          

        

        

          The octets in the score line are the library's own count: every delivery costs what
          wire or frames says the request weighs, so switching transports
          changes the arithmetic as well as the traffic.
        

      


      

        

        
03What the page shows

        

          

            aField order
            The head carries the host, then what the caller gave, then the defaults a request
              needs. Nothing is reordered to look tidy.
          

          

            bFraming
            HTTP/1.1 declares a length when the body has one and chunks it when it does not;
              HTTP/2 says the same thing with END_STREAM.
          

          

            cField sections
            The HEADERS frame is HPACK, so its octets are not text: the hex is what goes out on a
              first request, against the static table alone.
          

          

            dLoss
            A lost packet is recovered by the transport, not the library; what the library
              chooses is how many exchanges share one connection.
